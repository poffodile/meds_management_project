# Care One OS — clinical hazard log (DCB0129-style)

Working hazard log maintained during development. This is a **living engineering artefact to support** the formal clinical-risk process — it is **not** a Clinical Safety Case and does **not** by itself make the product clinically safe. A qualified **Clinical Safety Officer (CSO)** must own, approve and sign off clinical risk management (DCB0129); a deploying organisation carries DCB0160 duties. Populated/maintained by the `clinical-safety-reviewer` agent; risk ratings are **provisional** until a CSO reviews them.

**Risk rating** — record Severity × Likelihood → Initial risk, the controls, then Residual risk. Use your project's agreed 5×5 matrix (confirm the matrix with the CSO; do not invent thresholds). **No agreed matrix exists yet** — every Severity/Likelihood/Initial/Residual value below is provisional; do not treat any of these as sign-off.

## Handover to deploying organisations (DCB0160)

Record here any residual risks and safety controls that the **deploying care organisation** must operate. Items added from the 2026-07-16 Medication Round review (in addition to any prior entries — none previously recorded):

- **Witness verification (HAZ-05):** the software captures a free-text `witnessed_by` name typed by the administering user — it cannot verify a second qualified person was physically present. The organisation's own CD procedure/training must ensure genuine dual-signatory practice; the software control is not sufficient on its own.
- **Identity confirmation (HAZ-02):** because the record-commit modal does not itself show the resident's photo/name/DOB, staff must be trained to visually reconfirm the resident (via the surrounding page) immediately before submitting any outcome, until the modal is fixed.
- **Interim mitigation for silent-failure risk (HAZ-08):** until fixed, staff must be trained to independently verify a dose actually recorded (e.g. check the MAR/Missed Doses view) rather than trusting the Round page's apparent success, since a failed write is not currently visible. **Confirmed still necessary as of the 2026-07-16 write-path changes — this was not fixed.**
- **Connectivity (HAZ-13):** the page has no offline/sync safety net; the organisation must provide reliable network coverage at the point of care and have a paper/manual fallback procedure for connectivity outages.
- **Concurrency / staffing practice (HAZ-11):** the software does not prevent two staff recording *different* outcomes for the same dose slot concurrently (a partial DB-race fix landed 2026-07-16, but no conflict warning exists); the organisation's rostering/communication practice (e.g. one clearly-designated recorder per round) is a necessary compensating control.
- **Round-end policy (HAZ-10, HAZ-14):** until the software enforces a confirmation/escalation step, the organisation must set an explicit local policy for what staff must do before ending a round with outstanding doses, and must train staff on the recovery path (manager reopen) if a round is ended prematurely.
- **Training on PRN limits (HAZ-09):** the PRN daily-maximum/minimum-interval limits **now genuinely enforce** for the first time (previously they silently never fired). Staff who are used to this control never actually blocking them must be explicitly briefed that a real, unexplained-in-the-UI rejection can now occur, what it means, and what to do next for a resident who may need the medicine urgently (escalate to a prescriber/on-call clinician; do not treat the block as a system fault to be worked around by using a different page).
- **Historical "Asleep" records (HAZ-16):** the software's definition of "was this dose given" for the `S`/Asleep code has changed and been applied retroactively to existing records with no data migration. The organisation must be briefed that historical sleeping-related MAR entries may newly appear in a Missed Doses review queue, and must have a plan (audit sign-off, bulk review, or an agreed cut-off date) for handling that backlog rather than treating it as new clinical activity.
- **Do not use the "lab"/experimental Round page variants for live care (HAZ-17):** at least 13 additional, live, routed page variants (names containing "Lab" or "MedsRound4") still classify a sleeping (and mostly also a withheld) resident as having received their medicine — the opposite of the corrected pages. Until these are retired or fixed, the organisation must restrict staff access to the confirmed, current production Round page and must not treat any "lab"/numbered variant as suitable for recording real doses.
- **Stock figures may not reflect physical reality for some prescriptions (HAZ-15):** a prescription whose dose cannot be safely resolved to a single number (typically two-number liquid doses like "10 ml (500mg)", or per-site doses like "one spray each nostril") does not have its stock decremented automatically until a pharmacist maps it. The organisation must maintain a physical stock-check practice and must not rely solely on the system's stock figure for these medicines until the mapping exercise (audit DA-04) is complete.
- **Demo data (HAZ-21):** paediatric dose values in any demo dataset are explicitly unverified and must never be presented to a customer, clinician or auditor as clinically exemplary; the organisation/sales process must confirm demo environments are clearly labelled as such.
